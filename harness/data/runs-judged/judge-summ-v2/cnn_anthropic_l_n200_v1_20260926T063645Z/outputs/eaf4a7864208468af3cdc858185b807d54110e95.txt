Five young Chinese feminists detained in March face possible five-year prison terms on charges of "assembling a crowd to disturb public order," a lawyer for one of the women said. The women were held in Beijing, Guangzhou and Hangzhou shortly before planned International Women's Day events at which they intended to distribute anti-sexual harassment stickers. Their detention has sparked international protests, a "free the five" social media campaign and criticism from Hillary Clinton, prompting a rebuke from Beijing.
