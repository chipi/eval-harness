Opinion piece argues Obama's political stake in an Iran nuclear deal — central to his foreign policy legacy since his 2007 campaign pledge to meet Iranian leaders "without preconditions" — has left Washington appearing the more desperate party and weakened its negotiating position. The framework announced Thursday falls far short of Obama's 2012 vow that Iran would "end their nuclear program," allowing Tehran to keep about a third of its 6,000-plus centrifuges, retain facilities including a converted Fordow, and see sanctions eased almost immediately. Critics cite Iran's record of concealment from U.N. inspectors, Saudi threats to pursue nuclear weapons, and the war in Yemen as evidence the accord leaves Iran a nuclear threshold state with a one-year breakout period.
