Nearly 2,000 World War I soldiers' inscriptions have been discovered in an underground city beneath battlefields near Naours, France, about 120 miles north of Paris. National Geographic photographer Jeff Gusky, who is documenting the site, has recorded 1,821 names — about 40% Australian, most others British, 55 American and 662 untraced. The centuries-old quarry site was privately owned and off-limits until 2013, when a consortium of villages acquired operating rights to promote local history.
