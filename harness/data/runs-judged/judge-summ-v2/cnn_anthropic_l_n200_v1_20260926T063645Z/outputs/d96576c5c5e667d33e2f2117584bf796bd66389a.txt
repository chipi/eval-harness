Chinese prosecutors on Friday charged former security chief Zhou Yongkang, 72, with bribery, abuse of power and leaking state secrets — making him the highest-ranking Communist Party official ever to face corruption charges. A former member of the Politburo Standing Committee who retired in 2012, Zhou was expelled from the party and arrested last December; his trial will be held in Tianjin. Analysts call Zhou the biggest "tiger" snared in President Xi Jinping's anti-corruption drive, though some see the case as the product of factional struggle.
