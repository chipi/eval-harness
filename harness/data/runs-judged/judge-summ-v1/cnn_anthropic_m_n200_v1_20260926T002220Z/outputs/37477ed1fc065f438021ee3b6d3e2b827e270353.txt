A brief total lunar eclipse, lasting just 4 minutes and 43 seconds, occurred early Saturday, visible best in western North America and parts of South America, Asia and Russia. NASA called it the shortest total lunar eclipse of the century, caused by Earth's shadow engulfing the moon and often producing a reddish "blood moon" effect. It was the third in a series of four consecutive lunar eclipses, or "tetrad," with the final one set for September 28.
