Easter's Holy Week, culminating in the Easter Triduum (Good Friday through Easter Sunday), commemorates Jesus's crucifixion and resurrection while embodying a universal human pattern of suffering, transition, and renewal. The article explains that Christians experience this three-day cycle repeatedly throughout life—moments of darkness and loss followed by transformation and hope—making Easter's spiritual significance extend beyond a single calendar date. Unlike Christmas, Easter resists commercialization because it addresses fundamental aspects of the human condition that remain deeply meaningful to believers and non-believers alike.
