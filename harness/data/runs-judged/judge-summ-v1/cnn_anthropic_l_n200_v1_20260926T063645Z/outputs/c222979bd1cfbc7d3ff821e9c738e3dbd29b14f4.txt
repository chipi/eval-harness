Amnesty International's annual review found governments increasingly using the death penalty as a political response to crime and terrorism, with Pakistan, Jordan and Indonesia resuming or expanding executions and death sentences worldwide rising by more than 500 in 2014, largely due to Egypt and Nigeria. Despite this, recorded executions fell from 778 in 2013 to 607 in 2014, excluding China, where figures are a state secret. Amnesty notes 140 countries are now abolitionist in law or practice, up from eight in 1945, and argues there is no evidence executions deter crime more effectively than imprisonment.
