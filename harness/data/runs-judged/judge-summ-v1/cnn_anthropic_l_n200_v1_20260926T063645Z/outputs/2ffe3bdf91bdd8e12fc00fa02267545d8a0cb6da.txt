The Pentagon released a map showing coalition and Iraqi forces have retaken 25-30% of the Iraqi territory seized by ISIS, mainly in central and northern areas, though the Defense Department noted battle lines fluctuate daily.

Counterinsurgency specialist Afzal Ashraf told CNN the gains, notably in Tikrit, are significant because ISIS's claim to statehood makes territorial loss a blow to its prestige, but said Iraqi forces are relying on conventional frontal pushes rather than more effective "maneuverist" tactics they lack the technology to conduct.

Ashraf added that ISIS is offsetting losses with attacks elsewhere — including in Ramadi, at Yarmouk refugee camp and the destruction of antiquities — to sustain an image of success vital to recruitment.
