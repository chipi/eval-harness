The Taliban published an 11-page biography of founder Mullah Mohammed Omar, marking the 19th anniversary of a 1996 Kandahar assembly that pledged allegiance to him. Analysts say the document aims to counter rumours of Omar's death — it details his purported daily routine — and reassert his jihadi credentials as ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi claims the caliphate. The biography covers Omar's birth in 1960 and his mujahedeen campaigns against the Soviets, but omits US backing for the mujahedeen and the Taliban's alliance with Osama bin Laden. Omar has not been seen publicly since 2001; Washington offers a $10 million reward for his capture.
