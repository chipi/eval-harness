Rep. Ed Royce argues the Iran nuclear framework surrenders U.S. leverage built by congressional sanctions, leaving Tehran's nuclear infrastructure intact while releasing billions to the world's top state sponsor of terrorism. He faults the deal's 10-year limits, citing a letter from 367 members of Congress demanding constraints lasting decades, and the absence of "anytime, anywhere" IAEA inspections. Royce compares it to Clinton's 1994 North Korea agreement, which preceded Pyongyang's first nuclear test 12 years later.
