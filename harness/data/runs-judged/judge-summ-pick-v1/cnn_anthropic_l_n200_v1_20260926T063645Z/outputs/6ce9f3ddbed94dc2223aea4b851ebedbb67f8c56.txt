Chinese authorities have formally detained women's rights activist Wu Rongrong and four others for "creating disturbances" after they planned an International Women's Day protest against sexual harassment. By April 13 the five must be released or formally arrested, which in China almost always results in conviction. The detentions come as Beijing prepares an anti-domestic violence law and marks the 20th anniversary of the Fourth World Conference on Women, amid a broader crackdown on independent activism.
