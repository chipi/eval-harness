Indiana's new Religious Freedom Restoration Act has drawn criticism that it licenses discrimination against gay people. Twenty states now have versions of the 1993 federal law, but experts say claims under them are rare and successful cases have mostly involved religious minorities rather than Christian denominations. Past cases include a Native American's seized eagle feathers, a Brazilian church's hallucinogenic tea, an Orthodox Jewish inmate's kosher meals, a Sikh IRS worker's ceremonial dagger, and a failed zoning challenge by Chicago churches.
