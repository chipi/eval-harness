Amnesty International's annual report says governments are invoking real or perceived terrorist threats to justify executions, citing Pakistan's lifting of a moratorium after the Peshawar school attack and China's "Strike Hard" campaign in Xinjiang.

Executions worldwide fell about 22% to at least 607 in 2014, excluding China, where figures are a state secret and thousands are believed killed. But death sentences rose 28% to at least 2,466, driven by mass sentencings in Egypt and Nigeria.

Iran, Iraq and Saudi Arabia executed more people than the United States, the only country in the Americas to carry out executions, with 35 in 2014.
