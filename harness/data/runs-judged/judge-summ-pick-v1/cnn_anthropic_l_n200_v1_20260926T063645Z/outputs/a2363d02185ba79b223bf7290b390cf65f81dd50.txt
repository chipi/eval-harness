NASA scientists last week outlined plans to search for life beyond Earth over the coming decades, following the discovery of nearly 2,000 exoplanets since 1995. Astronomers estimate the Milky Way may hold some 40 billion Earth-like, habitable-zone planets, though only a tiny fraction would ever host radio-capable civilizations — and likely not at the same time as humans. NASA's James Webb Space Telescope, launching in 2018, will hunt for biological signatures in exoplanet atmospheres, while missions to Mars and Jupiter's moons Europa and Ganymede will probe for simpler life forms.
