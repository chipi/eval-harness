The NCAA says in a court filing it has no legal duty "to ensure the academic integrity of the courses offered to student-athletes," responding to a lawsuit by former University of North Carolina athletes caught in an 18-year academic fraud scheme involving fake classes. Critics, including whistleblower Mary Willingham and attorney Michael Hausfeld, say the stance contradicts the NCAA's longstanding claim that athletes are "students first" and are compensated with an education. The NCAA likened its role to that of the American Bar Association, while UNC, also sued, has admitted the fraud but asked that the case be dismissed as untimely.
