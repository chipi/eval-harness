Hillary Clinton's 2016 presidential campaign will inevitably become a barometer for gender equality in America, burdening her with expectations no male candidate faces. A victory risks creating complacency about women's broader political underrepresentation, while a loss could discourage future female candidates by perpetuating myths about women's electability. However, the author argues that partisan affiliation and economic conditions—not gender—will ultimately determine the election outcome, making extrapolations about women in politics from Clinton's experience inherently flawed.
