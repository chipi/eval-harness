Governments worldwide increasingly used capital punishment in 2014 to combat terrorism and crime, including China's mass sentencing in Xinjiang and Pakistan's resumption of civilian executions following the Taliban school attack. Despite this troubling trend, global executions dropped to 607 in 2014 from 778 the previous year, with 140 countries now abolitionist in law or practice. Amnesty International argues there is no evidence that execution deters crime more effectively than imprisonment.
