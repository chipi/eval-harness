The U.S. and five other world powers reached a framework agreement with Iran in Lausanne that significantly restricts Iran's nuclear program and effectively prevents it from building a nuclear weapon. The deal includes reduced uranium stockpiles, fewer centrifuges, and permanent IAEA inspection provisions, exceeding initial expectations. Critics' claims that the agreement contains fatal flaws—including sunset clauses, covert weapons programs, and insufficient restrictions—are contradicted by experts and the deal's actual terms.
