The Taliban released an 11-page biography of reclusive founder Mullah Mohammed Omar to counter rumors of his death and reassert his leadership amid competition from ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi. The document, published by the Taliban's Cultural Commission, describes Omar's daily activities and early life but provides no video or audio evidence of the one-eyed fugitive, who has not been publicly seen since the U.S. routed the Taliban from Afghanistan in 2001. Analysts suggest the biography is an attempt to maintain Omar's authority over Taliban militants who may be attracted to ISIS's more visible leadership.
