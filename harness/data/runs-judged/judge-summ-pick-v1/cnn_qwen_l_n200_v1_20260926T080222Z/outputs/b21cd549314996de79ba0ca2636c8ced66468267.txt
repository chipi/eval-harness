California's historic drought, now in its fourth year, threatens national food supplies and prices, as the state produces over a third of U.S. vegetables and nearly two-thirds of fruits and nuts. Mandatory water cuts are in place, and while some produce prices spiked in 2014, economists remain uncertain about 2015 impacts due to factors like a strong dollar and low oil prices. Meanwhile, Western drought conditions also strain beef production, though California wine quality has paradoxically improved.
