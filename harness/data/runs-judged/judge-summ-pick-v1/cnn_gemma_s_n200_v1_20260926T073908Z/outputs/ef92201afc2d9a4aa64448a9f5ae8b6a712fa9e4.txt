WW1 soldier graffiti from nearly 2,000 troops—primarily Australian and British—has been discovered in a vast, centuries-old underground city near Naours, France. The inscriptions, dating back to 1916, offer a poignant glimpse into the lives of soldiers seeking refuge near the front lines. Photographer Jeff Gusky is documenting the site, hoping to trace the identities of the 662 soldiers whose origins remain unknown.
