Easter, which celebrates Christ's crucifixion and resurrection, falls on a different date each year because its timing is tied to the first Sunday after the first full moon following the vernal equinox, a rule set at the Council of Nicaea in 325. Western and Orthodox Christians often celebrate on separate dates due to differing calendars, while secular traditions like the Easter bunny and colored eggs—likely brought to North America by German immigrants in the 1700s—remain popular trappings.
