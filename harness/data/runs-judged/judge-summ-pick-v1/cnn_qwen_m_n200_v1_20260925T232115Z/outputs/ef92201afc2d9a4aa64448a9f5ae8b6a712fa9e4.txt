Nearly 2,000 World War I soldier inscriptions have been discovered in a sealed underground quarry near Naours, France, offering a rare glimpse into the lives of troops stationed near the Battle of the Somme. Photographer Jeff Gusky documented the site after its 2013 transfer to local authorities, identifying names from Australian, British, and American units among the well-preserved graffiti. The find provides a poignant human connection to the conflict, with many soldiers recording their identities shortly before facing combat.
