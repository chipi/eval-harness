Amnesty International's annual report reveals that global executions fell in 2014, from 778 to 607, excluding China's undisclosed figures. However, several countries including Pakistan, Jordan and Indonesia expanded use of the death penalty, often citing terrorism or crime crackdowns, while Egypt and Nigeria drove a sharp rise in death sentences amid political instability. Amnesty argues there is no evidence executions deter crime and urges remaining states to abandon capital punishment.
