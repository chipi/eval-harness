California's four-year drought is a national concern, as the state produces more than a third of US vegetables and nearly two-thirds of its fruits and nuts; prices for berries, grapes, broccoli and other produce rose last year. Fresh fruit prices are projected to rise 2.5-3.5% this year, though a strong dollar and cheap oil may offset drought effects, while Western drought conditions drove beef and veal prices up 12.1% in 2014. Tourists face water served only on request, laundry restrictions and early ski resort closures — but vintners say drought years produce excellent wine.
