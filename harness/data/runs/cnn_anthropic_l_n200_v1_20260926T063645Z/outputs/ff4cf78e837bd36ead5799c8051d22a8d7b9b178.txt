Hillary Clinton is launching her 2016 presidential bid, entering the race as one of the Democrats' strongest candidates in decades, with deep political and policy experience and a proven ability to weather partisan attacks and scandals. But analysts say she faces significant vulnerabilities: doubts about her authenticity and trustworthiness, tepid enthusiasm among the party's liberal base, persistent gender bias, and the risk of running an "inevitability" campaign like Al Gore's in 2000. Bill Clinton, while a formidable campaigner, also poses liabilities, including questions over Clinton Foundation fundraising and renewed scrutiny of his personal life.
