Amnesty International's annual review found governments increasingly used the death penalty in 2014 as a political response to crime and terrorism, with Pakistan and Jordan ending moratoriums and Iran and Iraq executing people for "terrorism." Recorded death sentences rose by more than 500, driven largely by Egypt and Nigeria, though recorded executions fell from 778 to 607, excluding China, where figures remain a state secret. Amnesty says 140 countries are now abolitionist in law or practice, and argues there is no evidence executions deter crime more effectively than imprisonment.
