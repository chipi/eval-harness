In 84 days from November 2013 to January 2014, the area around Azle, Texas, shook with 27 magnitude two or greater earthquakes. Scientists believe these earthquakes were the result of high-pressure injection of drilling wastewater into the ground. The timing and location of the quakes correlates better to the drilling and injection than any other possible reason. This adds to other studies that linked injecting wastewater from energy wells to a tremendous jump in earthquakes in Oklahoma and southern Kansas.
