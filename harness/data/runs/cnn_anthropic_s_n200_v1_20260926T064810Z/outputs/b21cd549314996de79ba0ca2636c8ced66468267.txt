California's historic four-year drought is driving up grocery prices nationwide, with berries, grapes, and other produce rising significantly in cost since the state produces over a third of U.S. vegetables and two-thirds of fruits and nuts. Gov. Jerry Brown mandated a 25% water cutback, and while a strong dollar and low oil prices may offset some price increases this year, farmers' planting decisions could still impact supermarket costs. The Western drought also drove beef prices up 12.1% in 2014, though California wine production has paradoxically improved.
