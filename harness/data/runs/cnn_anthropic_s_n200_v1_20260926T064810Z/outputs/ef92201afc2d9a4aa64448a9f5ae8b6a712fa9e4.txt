Nearly 2,000 World War I soldiers' names and inscriptions have been discovered carved into the walls of an underground city near Naours, France, dating back almost a century. The site, which extends for miles and was used as a refuge by troops during WWI, remained largely unknown until the land changed hands in 2013 and a consortium of villages began promoting its historical significance. Photographer Jeff Gusky has documented 1,821 names—about 40% Australian, with the remainder mostly British and American—providing a poignant human connection to soldiers who often died shortly after leaving their marks.
