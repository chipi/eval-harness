Amnesty International's annual death penalty review found governments increasingly using capital punishment as a response to crime and terrorism, with Pakistan ending a six-year moratorium after the Peshawar school attack and Jordan and Indonesia resuming or expanding executions. Death sentences worldwide rose by more than 500 in 2014, driven largely by Egypt and Nigeria. However, recorded executions fell from 778 to 607, excluding China, where figures remain a state secret, and 140 states are now abolitionist in law or practice.
