Amnesty International's annual report found global executions dropped from 778 to 607 in 2014, excluding China, whose death penalty data remains secret. However, several countries including Pakistan, Jordan and Indonesia resumed or expanded executions, often citing terrorism or crime crackdowns, while death sentences rose sharply due to political instability in Egypt and Nigeria. Amnesty argues there is no evidence executions deter crime and urges governments still using capital punishment to abandon it.
