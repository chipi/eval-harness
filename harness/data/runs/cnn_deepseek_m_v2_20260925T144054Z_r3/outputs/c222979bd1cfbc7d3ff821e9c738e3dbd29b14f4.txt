Amnesty International's annual review found governments increasingly used the death penalty in 2014 as a misguided response to terrorism and crime, citing China's mass sentencing in Xinjiang, Pakistan's post-Peshawar executions, and Iran and Iraq's terrorism-related executions. Death sentences spiked by over 500 from the previous year, largely due to Egypt and Nigeria, though recorded executions fell from 778 to 607. Amnesty urged governments to abandon capital punishment, noting 140 states are now abolitionist in law or practice.
