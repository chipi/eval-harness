Amnesty International's annual review found governments increasingly using the death penalty as a political tool against crime and terrorism, with Pakistan ending a six-year moratorium after the Peshawar school attack, Jordan resuming executions after eight years, and Indonesia targeting drug traffickers. Death sentences worldwide rose by more than 500 in 2014, driven largely by Egypt and Nigeria.

Amnesty noted executions fell from 778 to 607 — excluding China, where figures are a state secret — and 140 states are now abolitionist in law or practice. The group says there is no evidence execution deters crime more effectively than imprisonment.
