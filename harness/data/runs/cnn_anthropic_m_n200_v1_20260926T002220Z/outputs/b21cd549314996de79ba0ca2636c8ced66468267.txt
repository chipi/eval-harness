California's fourth-year drought, which prompted Gov. Jerry Brown to order a mandatory 25% urban water cutback, is driving up US grocery prices since the state grows a third of the nation's vegetables and two-thirds of its fruits and nuts. Berries, grapes, broccoli and other produce rose in price last year, while a separate Texas-Oklahoma drought pushed beef prices up 12.1%; experts remain uncertain whether this year's impact will be as severe. Tourists visiting California will notice new water-saving measures like restaurants withholding water unless requested, though wine grape harvests have remained excellent despite the drought.
