**California’s historic drought—now in its fourth year—threatens U.S. food supplies, as the state produces over a third of the nation’s vegetables and two-thirds of its fruits and nuts. Mandatory water cuts have pushed grocery prices up, with berries, broccoli, and other staples rising sharply, though a strong dollar and lower oil costs may offset further hikes. The drought, affecting 52 million across the West, has also driven up beef prices and forced tourism-dependent businesses to adapt, though wine production remains strong.**
