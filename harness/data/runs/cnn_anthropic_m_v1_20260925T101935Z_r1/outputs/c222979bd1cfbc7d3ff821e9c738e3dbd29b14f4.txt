Amnesty International's annual report reveals that while global executions dropped from 778 to 607 in 2014, several governments increasingly used the death penalty as a misguided response to terrorism and crime, including Pakistan, Jordan, Indonesia, Iran and Iraq. Death sentences overall rose sharply, driven largely by political instability in Egypt and Nigeria, even as 140 countries have now abolished capital punishment in law or practice. The report argues there is no evidence executions deter crime more effectively than imprisonment, urging remaining states to abandon the practice.
