A surge in state-level Religious Freedom Restoration Acts, like Indiana’s new law, has sparked national debate over whether such measures enable discrimination against LGBTQ+ individuals. While legal claims under these laws are rare and often involve religious minorities—not mainstream Christian groups—key cases highlight tensions between religious practice and federal regulations, from Native American eagle feather use to sacramental tea in a Brazilian church. Courts have sometimes sided with religious claimants, but victories are inconsistent, and critics argue the laws offer limited real protection.
