Amnesty International's annual review found governments increasingly using capital punishment as a political response to crime and terrorism, with Pakistan ending a six-year moratorium after the Peshawar school attack and executing more than 60 people, Jordan resuming executions and Indonesia targeting drug traffickers. Death sentences worldwide rose by more than 500 in 2014, driven largely by Egypt and Nigeria.

However, recorded executions fell from 778 to 607, excluding China where figures are a state secret, and 140 states are now abolitionist in law or practice.
