Amnesty International's annual review finds governments increasingly using the death penalty in misguided responses to crime and terrorism, citing China's mass sentencing in Xinjiang, Pakistan's post-Peshawar executions, and Iran and Iraq's terrorism-related executions. Global executions fell from 778 in 2013 to 607 in 2014, excluding China, though death sentences spiked by over 500 due to Egypt and Nigeria. Amnesty urges leaders to abandon capital punishment, noting 140 states are now abolitionist.
