A nearly five-minute total lunar eclipse occurred early Wednesday morning across western North America, with the totality lasting just four minutes and 43 seconds—the shortest of the century. The eclipse was visible west of the Mississippi River and partially across the nation, though sunrise interrupted views on the East Coast; it was also visible in parts of South America, India, China and Russia. This is the third in a rare series of four consecutive lunar eclipses, known as a "tetrad," with the final eclipse occurring September 28.
