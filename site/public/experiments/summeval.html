Judging · SummEval

On mean τ, 23 of 24 LLM judges score above a trained free evaluator.

Grade machine summaries the way experts do. The best LLM judges reach 93% of a human expert's agreement (best of 24, same articles), far ahead of the free evaluator trained for the task (64%), which scores one summary at a time while the judges rank all 16 at once; and an open-weight judge is not separated from the leader.

The report's pick, as run here. Use an LLM judge, and start with gemma_l; before relying on any judge, check it against a small set of human judgements on your own outputs.

Question
Score 16 summaries of a news article on coherence, consistency, fluency and relevance, as three experts did.
Data
100 items from the SummEval test split
Systems
33 compared: 24 hosted models billed per call; 9 local models and baselines, free
Metric
Kendall tau vs experts — how well a judge's scores order the 16 summaries of an article the way the experts' do (Kendall τ-b per article, averaged over four dimensions); the field's standard. 1 is perfect agreement, 0 none
Calibration
rouge1 0.1487 vs 0.1500 published; rouge2 0.1578 vs 0.1610 published; rougeL 0.1435 vs 0.1280 published; unievalpub 0.3717 vs 0.3770 published
Billed
$7.03 for the whole sweep

03Findings

What this experiment found.

  1. On mean τ, 23 of 24 LLM judges score above a free evaluator trained for the task.

    The best free scorer, unievalpub (UniEval, on its own inputs), scores 0.3717; the weakest judge, llama_s, 0.3533. In the paired test UniEval is separated below 23 of the 24 judges and cannot be separated from the weakest, on either set of inputs. The judges rank all 16 summaries in one call while UniEval scores one at a time, and a pointwise LLM judge was not run.

  2. An open-weight judge is not separated from the leader.

    gemma_l scores 0.5384 against openai_l's 0.5398, and the leader cannot be separated from it or from 4 others. No provider was pinned: one judge, run once on each of two providers, scored 0.0113 apart and billed 3.7× apart.

  3. The dearest model finished 3rd of 33.

    anthropic_l billed $2.01 for 100 items. The cheapest model not separated from the leader, gemma_l, cost 72× less.

  4. The best judges come close to a single expert, and stay below one.

    Scored against the mean of two experts, as one expert is against the other two, the best judge reaches 0.5195 — 93% of one expert's 0.5587 (95% interval 89–97%), and the gap excludes zero. It is the best of 24, chosen on the same articles (92% when chosen on half and scored on the other half). UniEval reaches 64%, BARTScore 44%; a no-model lookup of which system wrote each summary reaches 63%. The summaries are 2019-era and reasoning was off.

  5. The order is stable; the winner is not.

    Two disjoint 20-article halves agree at ρ = 0.90 on the order of the 24 judges, but name the same unique winner only 21% of the time.

  6. This repository's own metric agrees with experts less than ROUGE does.

    coverage agrees with the experts at τ 0.1011, below ROUGE-1's 0.1487.

04Re-measured

These judges, put to work.

Two of these judges, gemma_l (τ 0.54) and qwen_s (0.52), re-measured the summarisation experiment's summaries, where its own metric agrees with the experts at τ 0.10. They re-ranked the field; one reader's blind check of 20 pairs went the other way, not significantly. On crowd ratings of 2022 LLM summaries (Zhang et al.) the same judges track people better than one rater does on coherence and relevance, trail on faithfulness, and do not rank the systems the same way; on the summarisation experiment's own outputs neither is confirmed.

05The method, here

Silver is not gold.

The best of the 24 judges agrees with the experts at τ 0.5398 — better than any free scorer measured, and, against two experts, 93% of one expert's agreement with the other two. An LLM judge is a silver reference. This experiment is what checking one against a gold set looks like.

06Reproduce it

Check it yourself.

The 9 free arms rerun on a laptop CPU for $0, pinned to the weights that were measured. The 24 hosted arms billed $7.03 when measured and rerun with one OpenRouter key.

make compare writes a replication card: each rerun against its committed run, item by item, and every separation and tie on this page re-tested with your reruns in place.

The guideThis example's code
$ make plan      EXP=summeval
$ make data      EXP=summeval
$ make reproduce EXP=summeval LEVEL=local
# with OPENROUTER_API_KEY set
$ make reproduce EXP=summeval LEVEL=hosted
$ make compare   EXP=summeval
