Classification · DBpedia-14

The leader cannot be separated from 19 others.

Fourteen-way ontology labels on a saturated benchmark: the leader cannot be separated from nineteen others (1 to 8 items behind), and the gold label is sometimes in dispute.

The report's pick, as run here. Take the cheapest arm you can run at 0.9857: gemma_m (~$12 per 1M items), qwen_s (~$26) or the fine-tuned bert_base_fy.

The goal's rule, in the catalog. Encyclopedia topic classification (DBpedia) (frugal, on correct) recommends bert_base_fy, the promoted baseline. 03Findings

What this experiment found.

  1. No winner: the leader and nineteen others.

    qwen_m leads at 0.9929, not separated from 19 others, including the free fine-tune.

  2. The dearest model finished 2nd of 28.

    anthropic_l billed $0.3784 for 280 items. The cheapest model not separated from the leader, gemma_m, cost 115× less.

  3. The benchmark is saturated, and the gold is sometimes disputed.

    Three items are disputed by 24 to 25 of 26 arms. Adjudicated by one reader shown the hosted answer, the gold was wrong on all three; the dataset's own gold stays primary.

04Re-measured

Re-measured against labels a person corrected.

One reader, the author, adjudicated 5 items, shown the hosted answer: the 3 disputed ones, which at least 90% of the hosted LLMs got wrong, and the 2 others bert_base_fy got wrong. The gold was wrong on all 3 disputed items and right on the 2. The top stays a crowd, and bert_base_fy moves from 3rd to 11th at an unchanged 0.9857. The dataset's own gold stays primary.

06Reproduce it

Check it yourself.

First, with nothing installed: test every arm against qwen_m, item by item, in the catalog's inspector, with the same paired test and Holm correction as this page; anthropic_l, the most expensive model, among them. The goal page has the rest: what the goal recommends, and why.

The 4 free arms rerun on a laptop CPU for $0, pinned to the weights that were measured. The 24 hosted arms billed $1.17 when measured and rerun with one OpenRouter key.

make compare writes a replication card: each rerun against its committed run, item by item, and every separation and tie on this page re-tested with your reruns in place.
