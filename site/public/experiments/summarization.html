03Findings

What this experiment found.

  1. A 1.6 GB model fine-tuned on this corpus ties the best paid model.

    bart_l scores 0.3461 on coverage, deepseek_m 0.3460 — a gap of 0.0001. It cannot be separated from 15 of the other 28 arms.

  2. The dearest model finished 18th of 29.

    anthropic_l billed $1.97 for 200 items. The cheapest model statistically tied with the leader, deepseek_s, cost 143× less.

  3. The same architecture trained on another corpus finishes last.

    BART fine-tuned on XSum's one-sentence BBC summaries scores 0.2071 — 29th of 29, below lead-3. What bart_l has is CNN's house style.

  4. The winner depends on the metric.

    On ROUGE-2 the three CNN-trained BARTs take the top three places; on ROUGE-1 the LLMs lead and bart_l is 12th.

  5. Scope: these are CNN's articles.

    All 200 come from CNN's 9% of the test set. On 200 DailyMail articles the five local models kept every verdict (bart_l 0.4430, lead-3 0.3697); the paid models were not re-run.

04Outside CNN

Does it hold on DailyMail?

The committed sample is all CNN. On 200 DailyMail articles drawn at random, the five local summarisers keep every verdict among themselves. The hosted models were not re-run there.

05The method, here

The data is the instrument.

The committed sample turned out to be all CNN's articles. A calibration row — copying the first three sentences on the full test set, against its published score — is what showed it.

06Reproduce it

Check it yourself.

The 5 free arms rerun on a laptop CPU for $0, pinned to the weights that were measured. The 24 hosted arms billed $5.74 when measured and rerun with one OpenRouter key.

make compare writes a replication card: each rerun against its committed run, item by item, and every separation and tie on this page re-tested with your reruns in place.
