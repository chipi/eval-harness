Retrieval · SciFact

Free retrieval first; a reranker adds about +0.07.

Retrieve the abstracts that support a scientific claim. The free e5_base retriever is not separated from the twelve LLM rerankers; reranking its top 20 adds about +0.07. The pick: build e5_base first.

The report's pick, as run here. Build e5_base retrieval first (0.7191, $0 per query); add an LLM reranker on its top 20 only if about +0.07 nDCG@10 is worth about $732 per 1M queries. Only the twelve cheapest hosted LLMs were tried.

The goal's rule, in the catalog. Evidence retrieval for scientific claims (SciFact) (frugal, on ndcg_10) recommends e5_base, the promoted baseline. The goal page, with its reasons →

03Findings

What this experiment found.

  1. LLM rerankers lead — and cannot be separated from eleven or twelve others.

    glm_s reranking BM25's top 20 scores 0.7437 nDCG@10; 12 arms cannot be separated from it, including the free retrievers e5_base and bge_small.

  2. The dearest model finished 2nd of 19.

    glm_m billed $0.4576 for 200 items. Only the twelve cheapest hosted models ran here, so this is the dearest of those; no Anthropic or OpenAI arm was run. The cheapest model not separated from the leader, deepseek_s, cost 7.6× less.

  3. The dev slice's winner finished 10th — inside a tie.

    Tuned on a 40-query dev slice, the leaderboard's winner there, llama_s, finished 10th of 19 on the 200-query measurement slice, 0.0265 behind the leader and not separated from it under Holm: a rank inside a tie, not a wrong pick shown at this n.

05Reproduce it

Check it yourself.

The 7 free arms rerun on a laptop CPU for $0, pinned to the weights that were measured. The 12 hosted arms billed $1.94 when measured and rerun with one OpenRouter key.

make compare writes a replication card: each rerun against its committed run, item by item, and every separation and tie on this page re-tested with your reruns in place.
